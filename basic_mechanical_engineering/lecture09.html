Lecture 9 · Mechanics of materials

Bending of beams

Shafts, axles, floor joists, crane girders and aircraft wings are all beams. Bending is the most common loading in machine design. It is also why an I-beam beats a solid bar of the same weight. Today we draw shear-force and bending-moment diagrams, compute area moments of inertia and find bending stresses.

After this lecture you can…

  • determine internal shear force V(x) and bending moment M(x) and draw their diagrams;
  • use the relations dM/dx = V, dV/dx = −q to check diagrams;
  • compute area moments of inertia of composite sections with the parallel-axis theorem;
  • compute bending stresses σ = M y / I and size a beam with the section modulus W.

Shear force and bending moment diagrams

Cut the beam at x. Equilibrium of the left part gives the internal shear force V(x) (sum of transverse forces to the left) and bending moment M(x) (sum of their moments about the cut). Sign convention: M > 0 for sagging (bottom fibres in tension), V > 0 when the left part is pushed up.

Area moments of inertia

Bending resistance depends on how far the material lies from the neutral axis, which passes through the centroid of the section. This is measured by the second moment of area:

Iz = ∫ y² dA   [mm⁴],    rectangle: bh³/12,   circle: πd⁴/64,    parallel axis: I = Ic + A d² section modulus W = I / ymax [mm³] · for composite sections: I = Σ (Ici + Ai di²), di measured from the overall centroid

Bending stresses

In pure bending, plane sections remain plane (Bernoulli). Strain, and therefore stress, varies linearly with the distance y from the neutral axis:

σ(y) = M y / I,    σmax = M / W,    curvature 1/ρ = M / (E I) design: σmax = Mmax/W ≤ σallow ⇒ Wrequired = Mmax/σallow

sizing a rectangular beam

A simply supported timber beam, L = 4 m, carries q = 3 kN/m. σallow = 10 MPa, height h = 2b. Find b.

Step 1: maximum momentMmax = qL²/8 = 3·16/8 = 6 kN·m = 6·10⁶ N·mm
Step 2: required section modulusW = M/σallow = 6·10⁶/10 = 6·10⁵ mm³
Step 3: dimensionsW = bh²/6 = b(2b)²/6 = 2b³/3 ⇒ b = ∛(9·10⁵) ≈ 96.5 mm, h ≈ 193 mm → choose 100 × 200 mm.
For the T-section, set M negative (hogging) and show that the tension side flips. The flange should sit on the tension side for brittle materials. Lab Test 10 verifies σ = My/I with strain gauges and also measures Poisson’s ratio.

Quick check

1. Where is the bending moment maximum in a simply supported beam?

dM/dx = V, so M has an extremum where V = 0. That is mid-span only for symmetric loads.

2. A rectangular beam b × h is turned from flat (h < b) to upright. Its bending stiffness changes by…

I = b h³/12. Swapping gives a ratio of (b h³)/(h b³) = (h/b)², so upright is stiffer by (b/h)² with the original h < b.

3. In pure bending, the stress at the neutral axis is…

σ = My/I with y = 0. That is why material near the neutral axis is “wasted”, and why I-beams and tubes are efficient.

Summary

  • V(x), M(x) from equilibrium of a cut part. dM/dx = V, dV/dx = −q.
  • I = ∫y² dA. Parallel axis I = Ic + A d². W = I/ymax.
  • σ = My/I, linear over the depth, zero at the neutral axis (centroid).
  • Design: W ≥ Mmax/σallow. Put material far from the neutral axis.
