Lecture 10 · Mechanics of materials

Torsion and power transmission

Every motor, gearbox, drill and drive shaft transmits power by torque. A shaft must neither break (strength) nor twist too much (stiffness). The theory of torsion of circular bars answers both questions, and it closes the course by combining kinematics (ω), dynamics (P = Tω) and mechanics of materials (τ, γ, G).

After this lecture you can…

  • derive and use τ = T r / J and φ = T L / (G J) for solid and hollow circular shafts;
  • explain why hollow shafts are more efficient than solid ones;
  • relate power, speed and torque, P = T ω, and size a shaft for strength and stiffness;
  • determine the shear modulus G from a torsion test (Lab Test 11).

Torsion of circular bars

Assumptions: circular cross-sections stay plane and circular, radii stay straight, and the material is linear-elastic. A line on the surface becomes a helix. The shear strain grows linearly with the radius, and so does the shear stress:

γ(r) = r dφ/dx,    τ(r) = T r / J,    τmax = T / W0,    φ = T L / (G J) polar moment of inertia: J = πD⁴/32 (solid), J = π(D⁴ − d⁴)/32 (hollow) · W0 = J/(D/2) · GJ = torsional stiffness

Why hollow shafts?

Material near the axis carries little stress (τ ∝ r), the same idea as the neutral axis in bending. Removing the core costs little strength but saves a lot of mass. For equal maximum shear stress and the same torque:

Dhollow = Dsolid / (1 − k⁴)1/3,    mass ratio = (1 − k²) / (1 − k⁴)2/3,    k = d/D

A motor delivers P = 15 kW at n = 1450 rpm. Steel shaft: τallow = 40 MPa, G = 80 GPa, θallow = 0.25 °/m. Find the diameter.

Step 1: torqueT = 9549 · 15 / 1450 = 98.8 N·m
Step 2: strengthd ≥ ∛(16 · 98 800 / (π · 40)) = ∛(12 580) ≈ 23.3 mm
Step 3: stiffnessθ = 0.25° /m = 4.363·10⁻⁶ rad/mm; d ≥ ⁴√(32 · 98 800 / (π · 80 000 · 4.363·10⁻⁶)) = ⁴√(2.884·10⁶) ≈ 41.2 mm
Step 4: decisionStiffness governs. Choose d = 42 mm (then check keyways and stress concentrations). Verify in the demo.
Message: reduce speed through a gearbox and the torque rises, so low-speed shafts must be thicker. Ask: “Why is a truck’s rear axle much thicker than the engine’s crankshaft journal?”

Quick check

1. A solid shaft’s diameter is doubled. For the same torque, the maximum shear stress…

τmax = 16T/(πD³), so 2³ = 8. The twist angle drops 2⁴ = 16×.

2. The same power is transmitted at double the speed. The torque…

P = Tω ⇒ T = P/ω.

3. Where is the shear stress zero in a solid shaft under torsion?

τ = Tr/J, so τ = 0 at r = 0. This is why hollow shafts are efficient.
