Lecture 3 · Kinematics

Kinematics of a particle

Kinematics is the geometry of motion: where a point is, how fast it moves and how quickly its velocity changes. We ignore the forces that cause the motion; they come in Lecture 5. Every cam, robot arm and vehicle trajectory starts here.

After this lecture you can…

  • relate position, velocity and acceleration by differentiation and integration;
  • determine the motion when the acceleration is given as a(t), a(x) or a(v);
  • analyse projectile motion using rectangular components;
  • split acceleration into tangential and normal components and use the radius of curvature.

Rectilinear motion: x, v, a

A particle moving along a straight line is described by its position x(t). Velocity is the rate of change of position, and acceleration is the rate of change of velocity:

v = dx/dt,    a = dv/dt = d²x/dt²,    a = v dv/dx geometrically: v is the slope of x(t), a is the slope of v(t). Areas: Δx = ∫v dt, Δv = ∫a dt

Uniformly accelerated motion (a = const):   v = v₀ + a t,   x = x₀ + v₀ t + ½ a t²,   v² = v₀² + 2a (x − x₀). These hold only when a is constant!
Ask students to predict the shape of v(t) before switching the preset. A classic mistake is using the constant-a formulas for the harmonic or drag case.

Determination of the motion of a particle

Usually acceleration comes from forces (Newton’s law). We then integrate to get velocity and position. The method depends on what a depends on:

GivenSeparate & integrateTypical source
a = f(t)dv = f(t) dt → v(t), then dx = v dtprescribed thrust, cam program
a = f(x)v dv = f(x) dx → v(x), then dt = dx / vspring force −kx
a = f(v)dt = dv / f(v)   or   dx = v dv / f(v)air / fluid drag

acceleration given as a function of time

A slider moves with a = 6t m/s². At t = 0: x₀ = 0, v₀ = 2 m/s. Find v and x at t = 2 s.

Step 1: integrate a(t)v = v₀ + ∫₀ᵗ 6t dt = 2 + 3t² → v(2) = 14 m/s
Step 2: integrate v(t)x = ∫₀ᵗ (2 + 3t²) dt = 2t + t³ → x(2) = 12 m
Step 3: why not v² = v₀² + 2aΔx?Because a is not constant here. That formula would give a wrong answer.

drag, a = −k v

A boat cuts its engine at v₀ = 8 m/s, with k = 0.5 s⁻¹. How far does it glide?

Solutionv dv/dx = −k v ⇒ dv = −k dx ⇒ v = v₀ − kx. The boat stops when v = 0: xmax = v₀/k = 16 m. In time: v = v₀e−kt, so it never quite stops, yet the distance is finite. Check it with the “drag” preset above.

Curvilinear motion: rectangular components and projectiles

In the plane, position is a vector r = xi + yj. Velocity v = dr/dt is always tangent to the path, and acceleration a = dv/dt in general is not. With rectangular components each direction can be handled separately. For a projectile (no air resistance):

ax = 0,   ay = −g  ⇒  x = v₀ cos θ · t,   y = h₀ + v₀ sin θ · t − ½ g t² flat ground: range R = v₀² sin 2θ / g, max height H = v₀² sin²θ / 2g

Tangential and normal components

Path coordinates are often more natural, e.g. a car on a curved road. The unit vector et is tangent to the path and en points toward the centre of curvature:

v = v et,    a = at et + an en,    at = dv/dt,    an = v²/ρ at changes the speed · an changes the direction · ρ = radius of curvature

Polar (radial–transverse) components are useful for rotating arms and orbits:
vr = ṙ,   vθ = r θ̇

Circular motion (ρ = r = const):
v = r ω,   at = r ε,   an = r ω² = v²/r.
We use these again for rigid bodies in Lecture 4.
Make the track a circle (a = b) with constant speed: at = 0 and a points to the centre. Then switch to the ellipse to show that an peaks where the curvature is largest, at the ends of the major axis.

Quick check

1. At the top of its trajectory, a projectile’s acceleration is…

The velocity is horizontal at the top (vy = 0), but the acceleration is always g downward.

2. A car drives round a bend at constant speed. Its acceleration is…

at = dv/dt = 0, but the direction changes, so an = v²/ρ ≠ 0.

3. Given a = −k x (a spring), which integration route do you use?

When a depends on x, use a = v dv/dx. This gives v² = v₀² − k(x² − x₀²).

Summary

  • v = dx/dt, a = dv/dt = v dv/dx. Slopes and areas link the graphs.
  • Constant-a formulas are a special case. Otherwise separate variables and integrate.
  • Projectile: independent x (uniform) and y (uniformly accelerated) motions.
  • n–t components: at = dv/dt, an = v²/ρ.
