Basic Mechanical Engineering: lectures

A proposal for running the 20-hour lecture of Basic Mechanical Engineering (course code W01-MEC-1SO-00667-00, semester 2, Mechanical Engineering, in English), with ten interactive lecture pages that can be projected in class and opened by students on their own devices.

The ten lectures

Each page has the theory, 3–5 interactive demonstrations, worked examples with step-by-step reveal, a quick-check quiz and a summary. The topics follow the syllabus “Contents – detailed selection” in order.

Proposed format of one lecture (90 min)

0–5
Hook: a real machine or failure that motivates the topic, plus the learning goals on screen
5–35
Concept block 1: board derivation of the key equation, then the interactive demo projected. Students predict before the lecturer moves a slider.
35–40
Quick check: one quiz question, phone vote or show of hands
40–70
Concept block 2 + worked example in exam style, revealed step by step
70–85
Concept block 3 / application demo, linked to the coming lab test
85–90
Summary and the remaining quick-check questions. Announce the next topic.

Teaching methods (matches the syllabus: blended learning, lecture)

  • Predict → observe → explain: students commit to an answer before the demo is changed. This is peer instruction for the quick checks.
  • Board + screen: derivations on the board, demos on the projector. The A+ button enlarges text for the back rows.
  • Lecturer notes (key T) contain timing, poll questions and common misconceptions. They are hidden by default, so the page can be projected safely.
  • After class: students open the same pages at home (offline-capable). They reproduce the worked examples and vary the parameters.
  • Exam style: every lecture has at least one worked example in the format of the written exam (task part).

Proposed semester schedule and alignment

I recommend running the 20 lecture hours front-loaded, one 90-minute lecture per week in weeks 1–10, so theory always precedes the tutorials (40 h) and the laboratory tests (25 h) that use it. Weeks 11–15 are left for tutorials, lab tests and the second tutorial test.

Exam: the written part has 3 tasks and 3 theoretical issues. Admission to the oral part requires positive task grades and at least 2 of 3 theory topics correct. In the oral part students explain and justify their written solutions. A tutorial grade of 4 exempts the student from the task part at the first attempt. The lecture quick checks and worked examples are designed as practice for both parts.

Learning outcomes covered: 1MEC1.1 (L1–L6), 1MEC1.2 (L7–L10), 1MEC3.1 (L3, L5), 1MEC3.2 (L4–L10), 1MEC6.1 (L1, L2, L7–L10), 1MEC6.2 (L7–L10, via lab links).

How to use the pages

  • Open index.html (this page) in any modern browser. No internet connection or installation is needed. All pages are plain HTML + JavaScript.
  • Keys: →/← next/previous section · T lecturer notes · P larger text for the projector · D dark mode.
  • For students: share the folder (e.g. via the GitHub repository, MS Teams or the university e-learning platform). The demos work on phones and tablets too.
  • Recommended literature: Beer & Johnston, Vector Mechanics for Engineers (Statics, Dynamics); Meriam & Kraige, Engineering Mechanics; Gere & Timoshenko, Mechanics of Materials; Hearn, Mechanics of Materials; Benham & Crawford, Mechanics of Engineering Materials. In Polish: Leyko, Mechanika ogólna; Niezgodziński, Wytrzymałość materiałów.
