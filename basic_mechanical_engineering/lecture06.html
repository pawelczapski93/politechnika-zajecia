Lecture 6 · Dynamics

Systems of particles and rigid-body kinetics

A rigid body is a system of particles whose distances are fixed. Two ideas carry us from the single particle to real machine parts. The centre of mass moves as if all mass and force were concentrated there, and the mass moment of inertia measures resistance to angular acceleration.

After this lecture you can…

  • locate the centre of mass of a system and use ΣFext = m aG;
  • compute mass moments of inertia using standard formulas and the parallel-axis theorem;
  • write the equations of plane motion ΣF = m aG, ΣMG = IG ε;
  • compute the kinetic energy of a rigid body and apply the work–energy principle.

Systems of particles and the centre of mass

For a system of particles with total mass m = Σmi, internal forces cancel in pairs (third law). Summing Newton’s law over all particles gives:

rG = Σmiri / m,    ΣFext = m aG,    L = mvG the centre of mass moves like a single particle loaded by the external forces only

Mass moment of inertia

Mass resists linear acceleration. The mass moment of inertia resists angular acceleration. It depends on how far the mass is placed from the axis:

I = ∫ r² dm = Σmi ri²   [kg·m²],    I = m k² (k – radius of gyration) Parallel-axis (Steiner) theorem:   IO = IG + m d²   (d = distance between parallel axes through O and G)
Body (axis through G)IG
slender rod, length L (⊥ rod)mL²/12
thin ring / hoop, radius RmR²
solid disc / cylinder, radius RmR²/2
solid sphere, radius R2mR²/5
thin spherical shell2mR²/3
rectangular plate a × b (⊥ plate)m(a² + b²)/12
Do not mix up the two moments of inertia. The mass moment of inertia I [kg·m²] (dynamics) and the area moment of inertia I [m⁴] (bending, Lecture 9) are different quantities with similar formulas. Both obey a parallel-axis theorem.

Equations of plane motion of a rigid body

ΣFx = m aGx,   ΣFy = m aGy,   ΣMG = IG ε fixed axis O: ΣMO = IO ε · rolling without slip: aG = εR

Example: a round body of radius R rolls without slipping down an incline. Friction F provides the torque:

mg sin θ − F = m a,   F R = IG a / R  ⇒  a = g sin θ / (1 + IG / mR²) the result does not depend on m or R, only on the shape factor IG/mR²

Kinetic energy and the work–energy principle

T = ½ m vG² + ½ IG ω²   (general plane motion),    T = ½ IO ω²   (rotation about fixed O) work–energy: T₁ + ΣU₁₋₂ = T₂ · work of a couple U = M Δφ · power P = F·v = M ω

disc rolling down a 1 m drop

A solid disc starts from rest and rolls without slipping down a height h = 1 m. Find its final speed.

Step 1: energy balancemgh = ½mv² + ½(½mR²)(v/R)² = ¾ mv²
Step 2: resultv = √(4gh/3) = √(4·9.81·1/3) = 3.62 m/s (a sliding block would reach √(2gh) = 4.43 m/s)
Step 3: does friction do work?No. The contact point is the ICR (v = 0), so static friction does no work, even though it is needed for rolling.
Gyroscopic effect (Lab Test 6). For a spinning rotor, the angular momentum H = I ω is large. A moment M applied perpendicular to H does not tip the rotor. It makes H precess with angular velocity Ω = M / (I ω). This is the same law ΣM = dH/dt as in Lecture 5, now for a rigid body.

Quick check

1. A bomb explodes in mid-air. Ignoring air drag, the centre of mass of the fragments…

Explosion forces are internal. Only gravity is external, so aG = g as before.

2. A slender rod (m, L) rotates about one end. Its moment of inertia is…

IO = IG + m(L/2)² = mL²/12 + mL²/4 = mL²/3.

3. A hoop and a disc of equal mass and radius roll from rest down the same incline. Which arrives first?

The disc has the smaller I/mR² (½ vs 1), so less energy goes into rotation and a is larger.

Summary

  • Centre of mass: rG = Σmiri/m. External forces only: ΣF = m aG.
  • I = Σ m r², I = m k². Parallel axes: IO = IG + m d².
  • Plane motion: ΣF = m aG, ΣMG = IG ε.
  • T = ½ m vG² + ½ IG ω². Work–energy: T₁ + U₁₋₂ = T₂.
