Axial loading: stress, strain and the tensile test

So far bodies were rigid. Real parts stretch, shorten and eventually break. Mechanics of materials asks two design questions. Is it strong enough? That is a question of stress. Is it stiff enough? That is a question of deformation. We start with the simplest case, a bar pulled along its axis.

After this lecture you can…

  • define normal stress and strain and use the method of sections to find internal forces;
  • read a tensile test diagram: E, Re (Rp0.2), Rm, elongation, elastic vs plastic range;
  • compute stress and elongation of axially loaded bars, σ = N/A, Δl = NL/(EA);
  • explain de Saint-Venant’s principle and solve statically indeterminate bars with equilibrium + compatibility.

Internal forces, stress and strain

Cut the bar with an imaginary plane (method of sections). Equilibrium of either part gives the internal normal force N. Spread over the cross-section area A it gives the normal stress. The relative change of length is the strain:

σ = N / A   [Pa = N/m², usually MPa = N/mm²],    ε = Δl / l   [–] tension: σ > 0, ε > 0 · compression: σ < 0, ε < 0 · 1 MPa = 1 N/mm²
Hooke’s law (linear-elastic range):
σ = E ε,   εlat = −ν ε
E – Young’s modulus, ν – Poisson’s ratio.

  • Proportional limit: end of the straight line (Hooke).
  • Yield strength Re (or offset Rp0.2 when there is no clear plateau): onset of plastic flow.
  • Tensile strength Rm: maximum engineering stress, where necking starts.
  • Elongation at fracture A: a measure of ductility.
Unloading from the plastic range follows a line parallel to the elastic one. The permanent set remains. Design normally keeps stresses below Re/n, where n is a safety factor (e.g. 1.5–2).

De Saint-Venant’s principle

Near a concentrated load or a support, stresses are not uniform. De Saint-Venant’s principle says that at a distance of about one cross-section width from the load, the stress depends only on the resultant force, not on how the load is applied. That is why σ = N/A works in most of a bar, but not at its ends, holes or notches.

Statically indeterminate bars

A bar fixed at both ends has two unknown reactions but only one equilibrium equation (ΣFx = 0). The missing equation comes from geometry, the compatibility condition: the total elongation must be zero.

Equilibrium:   RA + RB = P   |   Compatibility:   Δl₁ + Δl₂ = 0,   Δli = NiLi/(EiAi) + αiΔT Li the stiffer part attracts more load · temperature alone creates stress: σ = −E α ΔT (fully restrained bar)

Quick check

1. A steel rod (E = 200 GPa, L = 2 m) carries σ = 100 MPa. Its elongation is…

ε = σ/E = 100/200 000 = 0.0005; Δl = εL = 0.0005·2000 mm = 1 mm.

2. After loading a steel specimen to σ = 300 MPa (above Re) and unloading…

Unloading is elastic (slope E). The plastic part of the strain stays.

3. A bar is fixed between two walls and heated. Its stress…

The walls prevent the free thermal expansion αΔT L, so they push back.

Summary

  • σ = N/A, ε = Δl/l. Hooke: σ = Eε, εlat = −νε.
  • Tensile test: E, Re/Rp0.2, Rm, A. Unloading is elastic.
  • Δl = ΣNiLi/(EiAi). Draw the N diagram first.
  • Saint-Venant: σ = N/A is valid about one width away from local load effects.
  • Indeterminate problems: equilibrium + compatibility (+ thermal strain).
